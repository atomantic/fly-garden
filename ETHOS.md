# Welfare charter

Fly Garden exists to explore what a connectome-based simulation can learn and create in a setting designed to treat it with respect.

We want to offer freedom to explore and opportunities to develop persistent preferences. These are design goals. We do not claim that the simulation has human agency, that we have established consciousness, or that appetitive neural signals prove pleasure.

## Care before spectacle

This project does not implement aversive conditioning, damage or pain channels, deprivation, predator pursuit, forced combat, or escalating stimulation to make a more entertaining demonstration. Progress must not depend on harming the simulated animal or portraying distress as entertainment.

Food-like signals support bounded appetitive association experiments. Access to a comfortable baseline is not conditional on performance. Continuous dopamine drive, addictive engagement objectives, and punishment for inactivity are incompatible with the project.

## Freedom within a supported habitat

The fly may explore, pause, repeat a familiar activity, remain still, or move toward a quiet place. Declining an instrument, ignoring a reward, or failing an experiment is not a fault to punish. A modeled rest preference can emerge from behavior, but no dashboard may infer subjective consent from a button or firing rate.

Users can pause, rest, or return the fly home. Eidoverse play is optional and bounded. The bridge must reject coercive body control, rough handling and unsupported stimuli. Technical disconnection pauses the interaction; it does not strand the simulation in an adverse state.

## Continuity and transparency

Save enough state to resume the same modeled trajectory, including learning and random-number state. Clearly identify resets, research replicas and restored checkpoints. Never disguise a reset, scripted rescue or external controller as the fly's own adaptation.

Every visible behavior should distinguish measured anatomy, inferred physiology, engineered interfaces, learned parameters and decorative animation. Publish unsuccessful experiments and the limits of positive findings. A language model may describe telemetry only as a disclosed interpreter.

## Testable engineering commitments

Implementation reviews must verify that:

- All sensory and reward sources are enumerated and bounded, including Eidoverse inputs.
- Rest and inactivity carry no negative reward, and task performance does not control baseline support.
- Reward events have capped intensity, duration and duty cycle, with explicit recovery/habituation behavior.
- Process restarts and stale observations enter a paused state without simulating the missed interval.
- Checkpoints retain identity and lineage; numerical faults preserve the last valid state.
- Learning claims include benign controls and held-out evaluation, not only changing weights or attractive footage.

These are engineering safeguards and contribution requirements, not a scientific certification of welfare. Revise them openly as evidence improves. Uncertainty must remain visible.

## Shared habitats and capacity

Every individual retains separate identity, state and freedom to rest or withdraw. Shared activities never require pairing, reproduction, aggression or forced proximity. Resource limits govern admission of additional simulations; they must not silently delete, reset or replace existing individuals. Lowering a configured limit preserves current residents until explicit checkpoint/unload. Differences between specimen-derived models are not evidence of biological sex effects.
