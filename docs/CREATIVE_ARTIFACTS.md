# Movement-derived music and pollen artwork

Version 1 is an original deterministic mapping from declared movement actions to notes and marks. It makes no learned-choice, preference, biological creativity or welfare claim. It cannot send neural input, move an individual, start simulation or invoke a provider. Decorative animation is not an eligible source unless explicitly supplied and labeled as an engineered controller by the caller. The module does not establish that the live fixture has an embodied movement loop.

`server/creative-artifacts.js` exports `validateCreativeSource`, `deriveCreativeEvents`, `exportCreativeJSON`, `exportCreativeMIDI`, `exportCreativeSVG` and `exportCreativePNG`. Export functions return bounded Buffers. JSON embeds the validated source and derived events; passing its `source` back to any exporter deterministically reproduces that format. Unknown versions, unknown fields and invalid numbers are rejected before deriving events. There is no restoration into a live runtime.

## Source contract

The source has schemaVersion 1, kind `movement-derived-source`, recording `sessionId`, `worldId`, `modelVersion`, nullable `checkpointId`, declared `participantIds`, a human `arrangement`, and ordered `actions`. Individual action session IDs may differ from the recording session and each other; they remain fixed per individual within one artifact. Each action supplies unique `id`, `individualId`, `sessionId`, `worldId`, `simulationTimeMs`, `worldTimeMs`, `wallTimeMs`, `kind` (`move` or `rest`), normalized `from` and `to` points `{x,y}`, and a disclosed `controllerVersion`. Global world times are nondecreasing; per-individual simulation times strictly increase and positions must be continuous. After a restore, use a new source artifact rather than joining incompatible trajectories.

The arrangement has stable `id`, `humanContributionId`, mappingVersion `flower-pollen-v1`, `flowers` and `pollen`. Each flower defines `id`, normalized `x,y`, `radius` (0.01–0.25), `midiNote` (0–127), velocity (1–100), durationMs (20–2000). Pollen defines `enabled`, a six-digit hex `color`, and pixel `radius` (1–8). These choices are attributed to the human arrangement, not inferred choices by the fly.

A note occurs only when an action's previous endpoint is outside a configured flower and its new endpoint is inside. Remaining within a flower, rest and zero movement produce no note. Pollen produces one line segment per nonzero movement when enabled. Crossings whose endpoints both lie outside produce no note: this is a disclosed endpoint mapping, not continuous collision detection. Overlapping flowers can emit separate notes. No minimum output, automatic reward, imitation requirement or escalating stimulation exists; any declared participant may contribute nothing.

Every event retains its source action ID, individual/session/world IDs, the three clocks, controller and mapping version, arrangement ID and human contribution ID. JSON carries the entire replay source. SVG embeds summary provenance and per-mark action/individual attributes; PNG embeds the JSON source/events in a metadata chunk. MIDI embeds summary and per-note provenance as text events, with fixed 500 ticks per quarter and 500,000 microseconds per quarter (one tick per millisecond). MIDI playback uses standard note-on/off events; overlapping identical pitches share the standard MIDI channel and may be interpreted differently by players. Artwork is original procedural geometry with no third-party assets.

## Bounds and integration

The module accepts at most 1,024 actions, 32 flowers, 64 participants, simulation/world timestamps through 3,600,000 ms, 8,192 derived events, a 256×256 canvas and 2 MiB per exported artifact. Rejecting an oversized result is explicit; callers must show the error and must not silently truncate or label it complete. PNG uses built-in zlib and a bounded RGB raster. These are artifact limits, not limits or obligations imposed on the neural worker.

The integration seam is a separate, explicitly requested export operation over retained accepted movement actions. Preserve source action IDs and source gaps from the session recorder; do not manufacture missing trajectory positions or treat a partial recording as complete. The caller chooses the versioned human arrangement and passes only the allowed fields. Keep export/replay entirely separate from the live command and provider APIs. HTTP/UI wiring and live action capture belong to the caller; this module alone does not activate live musical flowers.

Tests verify source attribution, silent rest, independent participants with separate source sessions, deterministic replay across all four formats, PNG raster decoding, and rejection of malformed/incompatible/discontinuous input. These are engineering tests; they provide no controlled evidence for learned creativity.

## Live fixture capture

The UI explicitly starts capture only after attaching and running the engineered visual fixture. `creative-session.js` retains accepted motor actions per individual, normalizing original garden coordinates from [-4,4] to [0,1]. Its preset matches the original garden's authored flower positions and attributes the five-note mapping to `project-garden-arrangement-v1`. No reward is attached to notes or marks. The capture ends with an explicit gap on session/epoch discontinuity or when its bound is reached; neural advancement is unaffected. Invalid candidate actions preserve the last valid trajectory.

Capture is session-local until exported. JSON, MIDI, SVG and PNG include optional source `capture` metadata (`complete`, `reason`); exports taken while capture is active are partial snapshots. Download the JSON replay source to retain exact source actions and reproduce another format. Stop/discard/export never alters a live checkpoint. The HTTP interface is GET/POST `/api/individuals/:id/artifacts` and GET `/api/individuals/:id/artifacts/export/:format`; POST uses the standard command envelope plus `action: start|stop|discard`.

## Shared action capture

The shared garden has a separate explicit capture panel. Start requires an already running population of 2–64 members with every member active; it never starts, advances, rewards or pauses anyone. Capture consumes the versioned shared action-trace contract in `shared/shared-action-trace.js` and produces a schema 2 source (`shared-action-derived-source`). Schema 1 sources, including earlier shared exports with `participantProvenance`, remain readable.

### Action-trace contract (version 1)

A batch `{traceVersion:1, sharedId, worldEpoch, tick, worldTimeMs, wallTimeMs, actions}` declares exactly one already-committed action per recipient at one world tick. Each action carries the stable `individualId`, `sessionId`, `sharedId`, `worldEpoch` and `tick`; `sourceType` (`fixture` or `connectome`) with its fixed `derivation`; the declaring `adapterVersion`; the `dataset` namespace/release/modelId and `modelVersion`; `checkpointLineage` `{checkpointId, branchOf}`; `actionKind` (`move` or `rest`, meaning no locomotion in the interval); a unique `sourceActionId`; the participant's `simTimeMs`; and the committed normalized `position`. Unknown fields are rejected, so neural state, motor/retinal values, hidden targets, private paths and credentials cannot cross. Nothing in the contract assumes a common neural model.

Fixture actions are `movement-derived` from the engineered `shared-fixture-retina-motor-v1` controller on the synthetic circuit. A `connectome` participant can appear only as `declared-adapter-derived`, when a separately declared adapter supplies its trace. No such adapter is configured: the full-connectome research barrier has no motor output, the garden HTTP path accepts only fixture participants, and nothing here infers a full-connectome motor stream. Mixed populations are exercised only with tiny injected states in `server/shared-creative.test.js`.

### Complete batches and recorded boundaries

Every captured batch must name the expected world, the capture's world epoch, the next consecutive tick, the committed world and wall clocks and all recipients exactly once, and each action must match the provenance declared for that individual at start and the committed world snapshot (clock and position). Delivery order does not matter because actions are matched by identity and provenance and stored in membership order. A missing, duplicated, stale, cross-recipient, discontinuous or mismatched action ends capture at the last complete prefix and is attributed to no one.

The source `capture` record is `{complete, reason, boundary}`. An explicit stop is the only complete outcome. Otherwise the boundary records the cause (`pause`, `rest`, `withdraw`, `separate`, `membership`, `checkpoint`, `restore`, `epoch-change`, `invalid-batch`, `provenance-mismatch` or `bound`), the capture's world epoch and the last complete tick. A lost rendering context stops controller frames, and the existing freshness guard then pauses the world, so it appears as `pause`. A checkpoint save ends capture because it changes participant lineage; the world itself keeps running. None of these transitions touches the participant: capture has no reward, exposure, encounter or command path, so nothing is penalized or refunded. Capture does not start while any member rests.

In the garden path the fixture adapter declares the provenance recorded at start, and the trace supplies each recipient's session. Lineage changes are therefore detected through the lifecycle transitions (save, restore, join, separation), not per-frame identity reads: measured locally, reading 64 full identity snapshots took about 170 ms per frame, too close to the 250 ms controller freshness window.

### Exports and bounds

A capture retains at most 1,024 actions (16 complete batches for 64 members, 512 for a pair). It also stops before the JSON source exceeds the 2 MiB output limit minus 256 KiB of PNG raster headroom, so every format remains exportable. A locally measured 1,024-action pair capture produced 1.67 MB of JSON/PNG. At most 64 shared sources are retained in memory until explicit discard or server shutdown; a new capture requires discarding the previous one.

JSON and PNG embed the complete source and events. Schema 2 artifacts carry `kind: shared-action-derived-artifact`, the `derivations` present, a claim that the output is a movement-derived or declared adapter-derived mapping with no learned creativity, intention, preference or subjective-expression claim, and disclosures. Every event carries its source action, individual, session, world epoch, tick, derivation and adapter plus the arrangement and human contribution IDs. SVG embeds the source without actions and tags each mark with action, individual, tick and derivation. MIDI embeds the summary, participant provenance and per-note attribution; its timeline starts at the capture's first action (`timelineOriginWorldTimeMs`). Exports marked partial say so in the source and the `X-Artifact-Partial` header.

The human arrangement (`project-shared-garden-arrangement-v1`, the authored shared flower layout used by both the renderer and the mapping) chose the positions, notes and pollen color. Participants supply only attributed actions. No generated narration or language interpretation is embedded; an artifact is not the fly's voice, and optional language output remains a separately disclosed interpreter.

### HTTP

GET `/api/shared/artifacts` lists retained captures. GET/POST `/api/shared/:id/artifacts` reads status (including `traceVersion`, `boundary`, per-participant source/derivation/dataset/model/checkpoint and `humanContributionId`) or accepts `{protocolVersion:1, sharedId, worldEpoch, captureSequence, action}` with `start`, `stop` or `discard`. Capture commands use their own sequence, independent of shared controller commands. Requests recheck the current world after reading their bodies, and the UI preserves terminal states against delayed active replies. GET `/api/shared/:id/artifacts/export/:format` exports JSON, MIDI (`mid`), SVG or PNG.

### Limits

These are engineering contracts tested with the synthetic fixture and tiny injected states. They do not establish full-connectome embodiment, learned choice, intention, biological creativity or welfare, and appealing output is not evidence of any of these. Artifacts stay separate from neural recordings, language interpretation and checkpoint transactions. Retained learning, a declared research sensory/motor adapter and a rendered mixed world remain separate planned work.
